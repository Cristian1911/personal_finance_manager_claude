Why Oliva feels flat

The words aren't the problem. The shapes are: cards melt into the background, chip borders are too faint to read as "tappable", and "Vas bien" is a tint so pale it looks like an ordinary card. Everything sits in the same beige, so nothing leads.

What all three directions keep from Oliva: the hierarchy rule (black = you can tap it, color = how you're doing, gray = data), black rectangular buttons, round verdict pills, and one row grammar. What they change is where the state color lives, and the surfaces, borders and type around it.

Three directions

The timeline (S3-T): three ways to draw it

The same data in all three: last cycle, this cycle and the next, with paydays, bills, the Nu card's cut and due dates, and a big bill further ahead. Shown in direction B light so only the layout differs.

T1 · Carriles (lanes)

Like the diagram you liked. One lane per thing: paydays, bills, each card. You read when things happen at a glance. Weaker at how much.

T2 · Saldo proyectado (projected balance)

One line: your counted money over time. Solid up to today, dashed after. Paydays jump up, bills step down. Its lowest point is marked. Answers "¿me alcanza?" visually.

T3 · Agenda

A list by date with a running balance, grouped by cycle. The most readable on a small phone and the easiest to tap. Least "graphic".
