Why Oliva feels flat

The words aren't the problem. The shapes are: cards melt into the background, chip borders are too faint to read as "tappable", and "Vas bien" is a tint so pale it looks like an ordinary card. Everything sits in the same beige, so nothing leads.

What all three directions keep from Oliva: the hierarchy rule (black = you can tap it, color = how you're doing, gray = data), black rectangular buttons, round verdict pills, and one row grammar. What they change is where the state color lives, and the surfaces, borders and type around it.

Three directions

The timeline (S3-T): "Tu flujo", interactive

Built on the flow chart Zeta has today (plan-flow-chart.tsx): the balance line, daily bars around $0, and "Hoy". What changes: it follows your pay cycles instead of the calendar month, it projects the rest of the cycle and the next one (dashed line, outlined bars for bills still to pay), and you can touch it. Try it: drag across the chart to read the balance on any day, tap a day to see its movements and bills, and switch cycles. The theme toggles show it in both themes.

Tu flujo (full screen) · drag · tap · swipe

What it shows

  • Line: money in your counted accounts at the end of each day. Solid up to today, dashed after (projected with your usual daily spending).
  • Bars up: money in (salary, repayments).
  • Bars down: money out. Solid = already happened. Outlined = a bill or payment still to come. Gray = estimated daily spending.
  • Marker: the lowest point ahead, i.e. "will I make it?".
  • Touch: drag to read any day's balance; tap to list that day's items (each opens its detail sheet in the app).
  • Cycles: Pasado / Este / Próximo, or swipe. Totals under the chart follow the selected cycle.
  • Edges: 2 days before and after the cycle, shaded, so you see how it connects (e.g. the next salary arriving).
  • Below $0: the line and the area under $0 turn red, the day you'd run out is named, and a card says how much less to spend per day.
  • A big payment next cycle: an amber card on the current cycle warns early ("El próximo ciclo no alcanza…") with "Apartar" (S3-7).
  • How you get here: a "Tu flujo" row on Inicio with a small line and one sentence ("Llegas al 29 con ≈ $86.100", or the warning); tapping it opens this full screen. Every bill in Pagos also has "Ver en tu flujo".

One change from today's chart: everyday spending bars are neutral, not red. Red is reserved for "Te pasaste" (the hierarchy rule), so it keeps its meaning.

Inicio on a 2-column grid

The grid is back, simplified to two columns: each widget takes half or the full width. A collapsed widget shows only an icon, its title and one primary value or a small visual; details live in the expanded panel, which opens under its row and dims the rest. Urgent things call for attention: a colored edge and a short reason on the widget ("Mañana", "No llegas al 30"), and on opening the app the most critical one opens by itself. Try it: the scenario buttons at the top change what's urgent; "Organizar" changes size, order, and adds or removes widgets.

Every widget, every size it allows

This gallery is also the test: each widget at each size it allows, at the chosen width and theme, with its expanded panel. A size that doesn't give valuable information isn't allowed. In the app, the same gallery is a dev screen and is screenshot-tested (see below).

Rules for every widget

  • Answers one question no other widget answers; only the top block says "¿cómo voy?".
  • Has a minimum size: the smallest size where its information is still useful. It can't be made smaller.
  • Collapsed = icon + title + one primary value or visual (+ at most a 3-word hint). Everything else goes in the expanded panel.
  • Alert states: amber (needs attention soon) or red (critical), with a ≤4-word reason. The most critical opens automatically once per day.
  • Every widget expands (panel under its row) and has "Ver todo ›" for its full screen.
  • Amounts auto-fit their space; they're shortened ("$14,3 M") only when a full amount can't fit at 62% of its size.

How each widget is tested

  • Every allowed size × 3 widths (360, 390, 430) × 2 themes × light/dark × text size 100% and 130%.
  • Data cases: normal, empty (first week), long values ($14.250.000), negative, and the warning state.
  • Pass: nothing clipped or overlapping, contrast ≥ 4.5 for text and ≥ 3 for borders, and the main value readable in one glance.
  • Built as a dev "gallery" screen in the app with automated screenshots per case, checked on every change to a widget.
