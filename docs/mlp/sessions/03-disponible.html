How Disponible is calculated

The one number: how much you can still spend until your next payday. This page covers how each kind of movement affects it, with real numbers for cards, cuotas, loans, bills, Te deben and Tú debes, and when the verdict changes. The same function runs on the phone and the server. Decisions at the end.

The formula

Disponible = Llega este ciclo − Por pagar − Ahorro − Ya salió (± ajustes) Por día = Disponible ÷ días que faltan (incluye hoy), rounded down to $100

Llega este ciclo

Expected salary, replaced by the real amount once it arrives. Plus other income you received and repayments ("Te pagaron").

Por pagar

Bills, loan cuotas, credit card bills and money you owe people (Tú debes) that are due before next payday and not paid yet.

Ahorro

What you set aside this cycle. It's 0 unless you choose an amount.

Ya salió

Money that left the accounts that count for Disponible this cycle: debit and cash purchases, money lent, paid bills. Credit card purchases don't count here; the card bill does, in Por pagar.

What is a cycle

A cycle runs from a payday to the day before the next one. Paid on the 15th and 30th means two cycles a month: 15–29 and 30–14. If a payday falls on a weekend or holiday, the salary is expected the business day before. The cycle starts when the money actually arrives, or on the expected date with a "~" if it hasn't. With irregular income, a cycle is simply the calendar month.

The card only matters through its bill

Her Nu card's available credit is not money she has. It's how much more debt she could take on. Buying with the card only lowers that available credit. What touches Disponible is the bill. By default its minimum payment (S3-6): $640.000 due on 22 Sep, inside this cycle. The rest of the $1.850.000 she owes stays as card debt.

Credit card timing: when a card purchase reaches Disponible

Which accounts count for Disponible

You choose which accounts add up to your Disponible (Ajustes › Mis cuentas › "Cuenta para mi Disponible"). By default: checking, savings and cash count; investments don't. Credit cards and loans never count, because they're debt, not money you have. The first-day question "¿Cuánto tienes hoy?" is the total of the accounts that count.

The pattern: Disponible is the cash in the accounts you chose. Money already promised from them (bills, card bills, loan cuotas, debts to people) is subtracted before you pay it, so paying it never surprises your number. The card itself is debt, and it reaches your cash only through its bill.

The verdict: Vas bien, Cuidado, Te pasaste

One function, used for Disponible, límites and trips. It compares today's daily allowance with the one at the start of the cycle.

No flicker: the verdict gets worse immediately, but only improves after the better state has held for 24 hours. A refund that briefly lifts you back to "Vas bien" doesn't make the pill jump back and forth.

How we know it's right

The whole calculation is one pure function, computeDisponible, in @zeta/shared. It gets no database and no dates of its own; everything comes in as input. Every row of the tables above becomes an automated test, written before the code: 19 cases from the design docs plus the new ones from your decisions (partial bills, pay-off, Tú debes, cycle summaries). The phone and the server both run this exact function.

Decisions for this session

S3-0

Credit cards and which accounts count

DECIDED Your correction: a card's available credit is never Disponible, and card purchases don't lower Disponible when made; they lower the card's available credit. What counts is the card bill, in Por pagar of the cycle it's due. You choose the accounts that add up to Disponible; transfers between two counted accounts change nothing, transfers in or out of the counted set do.

S3-1 to S3-4

Cash, big card purchases, leftovers, Cuidado

DECIDED S3-1 An ATM withdrawal counts when made ("Efectivo y otros"); logging cash later only relabels it. S3-2 A card purchase ≥ $300.000 enters the projected card bill in full until you say how many cuotas (threshold editable). S3-3 Leftovers: ask; if unanswered, they stay available. S3-4 Cuidado at 85%, fixed, 24 h to improve.

S3-5

A growing card bill is never a surprise

DECIDED Same number, plus a look-ahead. Inicio shows each card's next bill as it grows ("Tarjeta Nu · próxima factura ≈ $480.000 · al corte ≈ $620.000 si sigues a este ritmo"), estimated from your card spending so far and your usual pace until the cut date. If that bill would push next cycle below 0, today's verdict turns Cuidado with the reason.

S3-6

Which card bill amount goes into Por pagar?

DECIDED The minimum payment, by default, because that's what most people pay. The rest stays as card debt (Pagos › Deudas) and its interest shows in the next bill. Later version: when a cycle has money left, suggest an extra payment with its real impact ("Si abonas $200.000, te ahorras $38.000 de intereses y terminas 4 meses antes").

S3-T

A timeline of your money

DECIDED You liked the timeline diagram. v2 gets a timeline view of how money moves across the last, current and next cycle: paydays, bills, card cuts and due dates, and the projected balance. Design variations come in session 5.

S3-7

How far ahead do you see bills?

DECIDED 60 days + big bills up to 12 months ahead, with "apartar". Bills over 30% of one cycle's income within 12 months appear as "Prepárate" with a per-cycle amount to set aside; accepting creates a reservation that works like Ahorro and is released when the bill is paid.

S3-8

Variable bills (EPM, Claro, card bills)

DECIDED Estimate first, real amount as soon as it's known. Average of the last 3 payments marked "≈"; card bills update live and become exact with the statement; utilities get a Revisar card on the usual arrival day ("¿Ya te llegó el recibo de EPM? ¿De cuánto?"). Biller emails via Gmail later.
